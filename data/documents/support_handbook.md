# Customer Support Handbook

## Purpose

This handbook defines how Ashwin Logistics customer support handles shipment questions, booking inquiries, documentation requests, cancellation questions, and claims-related intake. The support team is the front line for customer contact and must provide accurate, policy-based guidance while escalating operational or specialist matters when required.

The support model is built around three escalation levels:

- L1 — Customer Support
- L2 — Operations
- L3 — Specialist / Management

Support does not replace operational decision-making. Support follows internal policy and escalates to Operations or management when a case requires approval, operational intervention, or specialist review.

For claims related to cargo damage, loss, shortage, or service failure, Ashwin Logistics internal policy requires that claims must normally be reported within 7 calendar days of delivery or discovery of the incident.

## Support Principles

Customer support at Ashwin Logistics follows these principles:

1. Confirm the Shipment ID, Booking ID, or Customer ID before providing detailed guidance.
2. Use the current internal policy and status definitions rather than ad hoc explanations.
3. Explain status terms simply and consistently using the approved terminology.
4. Clearly distinguish between information-only support and cases requiring Operations approval.
5. Escalate to L2 or L3 when the issue involves operational disruption, serious claims, or unresolved service failures.
6. Do not promise outcomes that require specialist approval, especially cancellation approvals or policy exceptions.

## Ticket Classification

Support tickets are classified by the nature of the issue and the level of specialist intervention required.

### L1 — Customer Support

L1 handles:

- basic shipment questions
- status interpretation
- route and ETA clarifications
- documentation questions
- routine booking questions
- first-level claim intake

Examples:

- customer asks what BOOKED means
- customer asks whether a shipment is in IN_TRANSIT or AT_DESTINATION_FACILITY
- customer asks which shipping documents are required for an international booking
- customer asks whether a booking can be cancelled and needs guidance on the time windows

### L2 — Operations

L2 handles:

- delayed shipments
- failed pickups
- routing issues
- shipment exceptions
- operational intervention needs
- short-notice cancellation requests requiring approval

Examples:

- a shipment remains DELAYED and the customer wants an ETA update beyond the support standard
- a pickup fails because the loading site is unavailable or the carrier did not arrive
- a shipment is in EXCEPTION because of a route or document mismatch
- a cancellation request is less than 2 hours before scheduled pickup and requires Operations approval

### L3 — Specialist / Management

L3 handles:

- major customer-impacting incidents
- high-value shipment issues
- repeated service failures
- serious claims
- compliance issues
- unresolved L2 escalations

Examples:

- multiple missed pickups for a high-value customer within a single week
- an international shipment in CUSTOMS_HOLD for several business days without a resolution plan
- a serious claim involving cargo damage, loss, or repeat service failure
- an escalation that remains unresolved after L2 review

## Shipment Status Questions

Support should use the approved shipment lifecycle and status definitions consistently.

- BOOKED means the booking has been accepted and is scheduled for pickup.
- PICKUP_SCHEDULED means the pickup has been assigned.
- PICKED_UP means the shipment has been received by the carrier.
- IN_TRANSIT means movement is underway.
- AT_ORIGIN_FACILITY means the shipment has reached the origin handling point.
- AT_DESTINATION_FACILITY means the shipment has reached the destination handling point.
- OUT_FOR_DELIVERY means the final-mile carrier is dispatching the shipment.
- DELIVERED means the shipment reached the final destination.
- DELAYED, EXCEPTION, and CUSTOMS_HOLD are operational exceptions requiring investigation.

Support should not invent alternate statuses or describe a shipment as simply “stuck” without using the approved operational terminology.

## Cancellation Questions

Support must explain the cancellation rules precisely:

- cancellations more than 4 hours before scheduled pickup are allowed with no cancellation fee
- cancellations between 2 and 4 hours before scheduled pickup are allowed and the standard cancellation fee applies
- cancellations less than 2 hours before scheduled pickup require Operations approval
- after pickup, the shipment cannot be treated as a normal cancellation and must follow the return or exception process

Support must not promise a cancellation unless the booking timing and approval requirements have been confirmed.

## Claims Questions

Support may intake claims and provide basic guidance, but it does not determine final claim eligibility on its own. A claim should be logged, documented, and escalated to the relevant team.

The internal claim reporting window is:

Claims must normally be reported within 7 calendar days of delivery or discovery of the incident.

Support should ask for the shipment ID, issue nature, date of discovery, photos when applicable, and any proof of delivery or invoice information. If the claim involves damaged goods, lost cargo, shortage, or delivery exception, the case should be routed to Operations and then the claims team as needed.

## Documentation Questions

Support may answer general documentation questions, including whether a document is required, recommended, or supporting. However, support should refer customers to the Documentation and Compliance Guide for exact requirements. Common concerns include missing commercial invoice details, incomplete packing list data, customs paperwork, and Bill of Lading or Air Waybill requirements.

If a shipment is in CUSTOMS_HOLD due to incomplete or incorrect documentation, support must relay the issue to Operations and compliance stakeholders and provide a customer-facing update only after the information is confirmed.

## Communication Guidelines

Support interactions must be:

- clear and factual
- courteous and professional
- consistent with the policy language used in Ashwin Logistics documentation
- limited to validated operational facts
- careful about sensitive shipment details

When communicating with a customer, support should avoid speculation. If the cause is not yet confirmed, the response should say that the issue is under review and that the case has been escalated to the appropriate team.

### Examples of Good Support Responses

- “Your shipment SHP-1002 is currently IN_TRANSIT and there is no confirmed exception at this time. I have escalated the case to Operations for status confirmation.”
- “This booking can be cancelled because the request is more than 4 hours before the scheduled pickup. There is no cancellation fee in this window.”
- “A claim for a damaged shipment can be reported within 7 calendar days of delivery or identification of the damage. Please send the shipment ID, photos, and proof of delivery so the claim can be reviewed.”
- “The shipment is currently in CUSTOMS_HOLD, which means required documentation or clearance actions are pending. Operations and compliance are reviewing the issue.”

## Sensitive Information Handling

Support must protect confidential customer and shipment information. Only the minimum necessary details should be shared with the customer and other internal teams. Documentation, claim files, and shipment records should be handled according to Ashwin Logistics internal privacy and information governance practices.

Support should not disclose internal operational details to third parties without authorization. If a case involves compliance or high-impact vendor issues, L3 specialist review is required before broader disclosure.
