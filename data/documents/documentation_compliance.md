# Documentation and Compliance Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines the documentation standards used by Ashwin Logistics for domestic and international freight movements. It explains which documents are required for booking, transit, customs clearance, and final delivery, and it clarifies the difference between required documents and recommended supporting documents.

The goal of this guide is to reduce shipment delays, customs holds, and service exceptions caused by missing or inaccurate shipment records. It is an internal operational guide for Ashwin Logistics and is not presented as a legal interpretation of customs or import law.

For claims related to cargo damage, loss, shortage, or service failure, Ashwin Logistics internal policy requires that claims must normally be reported within 7 calendar days of delivery or discovery of the incident.

## Required Documentation vs Recommended Documentation

### Required Documentation

Required documentation must be complete and accurate before a shipment is released for movement or before customs processing continues. Missing or inaccurate required documents can trigger a shipment delay, documentation exception, or CUSTOMS_HOLD status.

### Recommended / Supporting Documentation

Recommended or supporting documentation helps improve traceability, customer communication, and exception resolution. It is useful when a shipment requires verification or when the customer requests more detail about the cargo or route.

## Domestic Shipment Documentation

For domestic shipments, Ashwin Logistics normally requires the documents below as part of a complete operational file:

- customer booking confirmation
- shipment ID and booking reference
- origin and destination address information
- commercial invoice or sales document for billing and merchandise identification
- packing list for item-level content reference
- delivery instructions and contact details
- proof of collection or pickup confirmation
- proof of delivery for final handoff

The commercial invoice and packing list are often essential even for domestic shipments because they provide the data needed to validate shipment contents, support claims, and confirm the expected quantity and packaging.

## International Shipment Documentation

International shipments require a more complete documentation package. The required set typically includes:

- commercial invoice
- packing list
- Bill of Lading for sea freight shipments
- Air Waybill for air freight shipments
- license or permit references, when applicable
- customs declaration information
- importer or exporter details
- country-specific compliance requirements as defined in the booking record

### Commercial Invoice

A commercial invoice records the cargo description, declared value, importer and exporter information, and terms of sale. This document is essential for customs processing, prior to release of cargo, and for downstream claims or dispute review.

### Packing List

The packing list provides details of the contents, box or pallet count, dimensions, weights, and packaging arrangement. It supports verification of shipment quantity and helps prevent shortages, misdeclarations, and missing contents claims.

### Bill of Lading

For sea freight, the Bill of Lading is the primary transport document that defines shipment terms, cargo identity, and destination handling. It is required for freight movement and for custody or release verification at the destination.

### Air Waybill

For air freight, the Air Waybill is the standard transport document used to identify the shipment and confirm the air carrier’s responsibility for movement. It is required for air freight processing and should match the booking details and customs data.

## Customs Documentation and Compliance

Ashwin Logistics documentation teams and compliance stakeholders use the following guidelines for customs-related shipments:

- verify the commercial invoice value and description against the packing list
- verify the consignee, shipper, and country details before release
- ensure the declared goods description matches the booking and service instructions
- confirm any permits, licenses, or product-specific requirements before transit begins
- maintain a complete record of documents for customs review and post-shipment inquiries

If any document is missing, incorrect, or inconsistent, the shipment may move to CUSTOMS_HOLD. A shipment in CUSTOMS_HOLD means the shipment cannot proceed through the applicable customs process until the required documentation or clearance action is completed.

## Document Verification Process

Ashwin Logistics follows a verification procedure before shipment release and during transshipment events:

1. Check that all required documents are present.
2. Validate that customer and routing information matches the booking record.
3. Confirm the cargo description and declared value align with the commercial invoice and packing list.
4. Review shipment references such as booking number, Shipment ID, and destination details.
5. Request additional documents when required for customs or warehouse handling.
6. Escalate to compliance or Operations if discrepancies remain unresolved.

## Missing or Incorrect Documentation

A missing document is any required shipment file item that is absent or incomplete. An incorrect document is any document that contains conflicting, mismatched, or inaccurate information.

Examples include:

- missing commercial invoice for an international shipment
- packing list showing a different item count than the booking record
- wrong consignee information on the Bill of Lading
- incorrect declared value on a customs file
- mismatch between the Air Waybill and final destination details

When a document issue is identified, the operations or compliance team should document the discrepancy and request a corrected file before the shipment continues. If the issue is material to clearance, the shipment may be placed in CUSTOMS_HOLD.

## Recommended and Supporting Documents

Recommended or supporting documentation may include:

- customer purchase order or contract reference
- product safety or handling certificates
- weight tickets or dimension reports
- special handling instructions
- insurance or declared value confirmation
- customs broker communication notes
- warehouse receiving or dispatch notes

These documents support traceability and customer communication but are not always mandatory for all shipments. They are especially useful when the shipment is high value, perishable, hazardous, or subject to repeated compliance checks.

## Escalation and Responsibilities

### L1 Support Responsibilities

- answer basic questions about document requirements
- identify whether a document is required or recommended
- route missing or incorrect documentation issues to Operations or compliance teams

### L2 Operations Responsibilities

- verify transit paperwork and pickup documentation
- resolve routing or warehouse-related document issues
- monitor whether a missing document has triggered a shipment exception or customs hold

### L3 Specialist / Management Responsibilities

- oversee major compliance issues
- resolve unresolved customs-related gaps
- manage repeated documentation failures or customer-impacting compliance events
