# Claims and Damaged Shipment Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how Ashwin Logistics reviews and resolves claims for damaged, delayed, lost, or incorrectly delivered shipments. It applies to both domestic and international shipments carried under Ashwin Logistics service contracts, warehousing arrangements, and distribution programs.

A claim is assigned a unique identifier in the format CLM-XXXX. Examples include CLM-4001 and CLM-4002.

## Claim Types

Ashwin Logistics recognizes the following claim categories:

- Damaged shipment
- Lost shipment
- Missing contents
- Shortage
- Concealed damage
- Delivery exception

### Definition of Claim Types

- Damaged shipment: the goods arrived with physical damage to packaging or product.
- Lost shipment: the shipment cannot be located or has not reached the intended destination within the service expectations.
- Missing contents: the package arrived but one or more items were not included.
- Shortage: the quantity delivered is less than the quantity recorded on the shipment documents.
- Concealed damage: the outer packaging appears acceptable but damage is found after opening or unpacking.
- Delivery exception: the shipment was not delivered as expected because of address, access, refusal, or service disruption issues that created a loss or service failure.

## Claim Eligibility

Claims are eligible when the shipment was accepted by Ashwin Logistics, the incident occurred during the service period, and the customer reports the issue within the internal reporting window.

Ashwin Logistics applies the following internal rule:

Claims must normally be reported within 7 calendar days of delivery or discovery of the incident.

This is an internal policy for Ashwin Logistics operations and customer support. It is not a legal determination and does not replace any contractual agreement between Ashwin Logistics and the customer.

### Excluded Scenarios

Claims may be denied or limited when:

- the shipment was accepted without defects and no damage was noted at delivery
- the loss or damage arose from packaging not suitable for the cargo type
- the customer had not followed documented handling instructions
- the claim is submitted after the standard internal reporting window without approved exception
- the issue is not supported by evidence or required documentation

## Required Evidence

Customers or internal teams must provide evidence sufficient to establish the nature of the claim. The minimum evidence expected for a standard claim includes:

- shipment ID, such as SHP-1002
- booking ID, if available
- delivery date and delivery location
- photos of damaged packaging or product, when relevant
- packing list or invoice where applicable
- proof of shortage or content mismatch
- written customer description of the incident
- the name and role of the person reporting the claim

For concealed damage cases, the customer must provide evidence that the damage was discovered after delivery and not visible at the time of receipt. For lost shipment claims, the customer may also need to provide recent tracking history and any delivery confirmation references.

## Required Documents

The following documents may be required depending on the claim type:

- commercial invoice
- packing list
- Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, where relevant
- proof of delivery or delivery exception report
- damage photographs
- forwarding instructions or customer order confirmation
- customs documentation for international claims

The documentation package should be complete enough for the claims team to verify the shipment contents, condition, and service performance.

## Claim Submission Procedure

1. The customer or support representative identifies the claim type and shipment ID.
2. The issue is logged in the claims register with a unique CLM-XXXX identifier.
3. The support team collects the required evidence and confirms the report date.
4. The case is routed to the appropriate operations or claims team owner.
5. The claims team verifies the evidence, service timeline, and shipment condition.
6. The claims team communicates a provisional decision or requests additional information.

### Support Responsibility

Customer support is responsible for:

- documenting the initial complaint clearly
- confirming the shipment ID and booking reference
- collecting basic evidence and contact details
- identifying whether the claim falls within the 7-day reporting window
- escalating to Operations when there is a shipment loss, delivery exception, or service disruption

### Operations Responsibility

Operations is responsible for:

- validating the physical movement history of the shipment
- confirming route status, pickup records, and delivery details
- reviewing exception records, delay records, or failed delivery notes
- coordinating with warehouse or transport teams for damaged or missing cargo cases
- supporting the claims team with operational evidence

### Claims Team Responsibility

The claims team is responsible for:

- assessing claim eligibility
- reviewing supporting documentation
- determining whether the claim is approved, partially approved, denied, or escalated
- communicating the outcome to the customer and support representatives
- escalating to management when the claim is high-value, repeated, or materially impacts customer trust

## Claim Review and Customer Communication

Claims are reviewed using a standard procedure to keep decision-making consistent. The claims team evaluates the facts, the evidence provided, the service records, and any documentation gaps.

Customer communication should be factual and timely. The claim owner should explain:

- what the issue is
- what evidence is required
- when a decision is expected
- whether the case is under review or requires additional documentation

If the claim is missing required documents, the support or claims team must ask for the missing information without delaying resolution unnecessarily.

## Escalation Process

Claims typically remain with L1 support only for intake and triage. They move to L2 when Operations needs to validate the shipment record or investigate an exception. They move to L3 when the claim is high-value, recurring, or involves serious customer-impacting issues, compliance concerns, or unresolved disputes.

Examples of major claim escalations include:

- repeated shortage claims across multiple shipments for the same customer
- a high-value cargo damage claim with limited evidence
- a shipment lost during international transit with incomplete documentation
- a delivery exception causing extensive downstream service disruption

## Examples

### Example 1: Damaged Shipment

A customer receives SHP-1010 and reports cracked packaging and broken goods on the third day after delivery. The case is logged as a damaged shipment claim and assigned CLM-4001. The customer provides photos, the delivery form, and the packing list. The claims team reviews the evidence and verifies the damage was discovered within the 7-day reporting period.

### Example 2: Shortage

A customer reports that SHP-1014 arrived with fewer cartons than listed on the packing list. The case is logged as a shortage claim. The support team requests the packing list, invoice, and proof of delivery. The operations team confirms the quantity discrepancy and the claim proceeds to review.

### Example 3: Concealed Damage

A customer receives a pallet in good condition but discovers product damage after opening the shipment. The claim is classified as concealed damage. The customer must provide photos and a description of the defect and when it was discovered. Because the damage was found after delivery, the case is reviewed under the internal claim rules for concealed damage.

### Example 4: Lost Shipment

A domestic shipment enters EXCEPTION after route irregularities and cannot be located for several days. The customer reports the situation as a lost shipment claim. The claims team requests the shipment ID, tracking history, and supporting documentation and escalates to Operations for investigation.
